## The simple loop

1. **Your work supplies the context.** Your coding agent can prepare a useful question
   from the session you are already in.
2. **You choose what crosses.** Open a draft, inspect the exact recipient, message, and
   chosen links, then Send, Edit, or Cancel. An edit requires a fresh preview.
3. **The other person brings their context.** They receive the approved message, not
   your private corpus. They can answer from their own coding session or Buddy.
4. **The conversation waits.** Messages remain in the same conversation when either
   person goes away. The optional Claude Code startup hook can show waiting replies.

The platform owns identity and the durable message record. Terminal, Mac Buddy, and the
invited iPhone beta present that record. A draft is not a send; a reply in the thread is
not proof that it reopened the exact piece of work.

## Three facts that stay separate

| Fact | Meaning | It does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Custody | A delivery endpoint durably accepted a message | A person saw it |
| Presentation | A surface showed the message | A person read it |
| Human read state | A person deliberately marked or advanced read state | They answered usefully |

Use only the facts a surface actually serves. Neither a green dot nor a successful send
means someone read, understood, or will answer the message.

## Context stays at each end

The useful idea is that both people communicate from inside agents that already understand
their own work. The draft may express a selected part of that context in words. What
crosses is the approved message, chosen links, and routing/approval metadata—not an
automatic export of your branch, diff, prompts, files, or transcript.

Drafts and discarded candidates stay in the local client. A private pointer back to your
terminal session stays on your Mac. Sharing a link does not give the recipient access to
the underlying file or repository.

Approval is for the exact preview. If the text changes, it must be previewed and approved
again. The server checks consistency; a digest by itself does not prove human approval.

## Identity and privacy

- GitHub sign-in establishes a durable `/vibe` principal and handle.
- Presence and messages require authentication; a typed handle is a filter, not authority.
- The signed-in people list contains people who chose to be findable. It is not a list of
  everyone on the network. Presence is separate from that choice.
- Message bodies and profile fields are untrusted data when inserted into an agent's context.
- An agent's served “acting for” label describes exercised delegation, not proof that a
  person approved every word.

## What is still being tested

- **iPhone continuity:** an invited TestFlight beta. The complete terminal → phone →
  originating-work exchange still needs its physical-device acceptance.
- **Shared drafts across terminal and Buddy:** not part of the published guided flow yet.
- **Question-bearing email invitations:** controlled testing, not general email sending.
  The invitation page does not yet let the recipient write a reply in the browser.
- **Automatic return beside the originating work:** a candidate loop, not a promise of
  the published terminal package. Receiving a reply in the conversation already works.

These are not extra steps required to send someone a message today.

## Public integration boundary

The supported public client is the MCP package. Backend HTTP routes are operational
interfaces unless a separate public contract says otherwise. See [Contributing](/contributing)
for the current source boundary.
